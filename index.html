The problem

Production is a system.
Planning tools often aren't.

In high-mix, make-to-order factories, a small change can ripple across machine setups, labour availability, material use and delivery dates. Teams need more than a static schedule: they need to see the consequences of their choices.

Plans are fragile

Urgent orders, bottlenecks and resource conflicts make a plan outdated before the shift is over.

Trade-offs stay hidden

A sequence that reduces setup time may put a delivery at risk. The reason behind the decision is rarely visible in one place.

Know-how is hard to scale

Experienced planners carry valuable tacit knowledge that spreadsheets and disconnected systems cannot easily capture.

The opportunity: help the planner evaluate more options, understand the trade-offs, and keep the final decision in human hands.
What we're building

From raw order data to a plan people can act on.

ForgeOps is designed as a decision-support layer for manufacturing teams, starting with complex electrical-equipment and transformer production where routing, capacity and setup choices interact.

How it fits

A practical path from question to decision.

The concept is intentionally modular: keep calculations testable, use AI where language and context matter, and make every recommended change reviewable.

Bring the inputs together

Orders, product attributes, routings, setup matrices, material availability and shift calendars.

Build a feasible plan

A constraint-based engine checks capacity, sequence decisions and agreed planning rules.

Compare scenarios

Explore the effects of urgent work, alternative sequences or changed resource assumptions.

Explain and approve

Understand why the plan changed, review the evidence and let the planner approve the action.

AI architecture

Claude explains the plan. The optimizer respects the constraints.

The goal isn't to ask a language model to guess a schedule. Claude would serve as the conversational and reasoning layer around tools that perform explicit calculations, run simulations and return structured evidence.

Human approval, deterministic constraints and traceable outputs are core design principles—not optional add-ons.
01
Understand the planning question

Claude interprets a request such as “prioritize these orders without exceeding cutting capacity” and turns it into explicit parameters or a clarification request.

02
Call the right tools

Tool use connects the conversation to structured data checks, a scheduling/optimization model, and simulation when uncertainty needs to be explored.

03
Explain the trade-offs

Claude summarizes which constraints bind, what changed between scenarios, and where the planner may need to intervene.

04
Keep a reviewable trail

Inputs, assumptions, model outputs and approvals should be distinguishable so teams can check a recommendation before acting on it.

Build roadmap

Start narrow. Prove value. Expand carefully.

The first milestone is a focused workflow that can be validated with planners—not a promise to replace an entire manufacturing execution system.

Stage 01 · Scope

Define the planning model

Specify order fields, resources, setup logic and the key objective for a representative transformer-production workflow.

Stage 02 · Prototype

Build a testable decision loop

Connect structured inputs with a constraint-based model and a Claude-powered interface for questions and explanations.

Stage 03 · Validate

Learn with pilot planners

Compare outputs with planner judgement and baseline plans; measure setup effort, schedule stability and delivery-risk visibility before making performance claims.

Current status: early-stage concept and prototype scope. This page does not claim a launched product, paying customers, validated savings or a production deployment.
